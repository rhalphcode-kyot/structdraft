Lesson Discussion

What is Dimensioning?

Dimensioning is the process of showing the size, distance, location, or position of an object on a technical drawing. Dimensions provide the measurements needed to understand the actual size of structural elements.

Basic Parts of Dimensioning

1. Dimension Line A line that shows the distance or measurement between two points. It usually has arrowheads at both ends.

2. Extension Line A thin line that extends from the object to indicate the limits of a measurement.

3. Dimension Text/Figure The number that indicates the actual measurement.

4. ArrowheadsMarks placed at the ends of a dimension line to indicate the limits of the measurement.

5. Leader Line A line used to connect a note or dimension to a specific feature of an object.

Types of Basic Dimensions

Linear Dimension Shows a straight distance such as length, width, or height.

Angular Dimension Shows the measurement of an angle, usually expressed in degrees.

Diameter Dimension Shows the diameter of a circular feature and is commonly identified by the Ø symbol.

Radius Dimension Shows the distance from the center of a circle or arc to its edge and is commonly identified by R.

Importance of Dimensioning

Proper dimensioning helps:

Communicate accurate measurements.

Show the size and location of structural components.

Reduce misunderstandings when reading drawings.

Provide necessary information for construction.

Keep drawings clear and organized.

Lesson Discussion

What is Reading a Structural Drawing?

Reading a structural drawing means understanding the symbols, lines, dimensions, notes, and structural elements shown in a technical drawing.

A structural drawing communicates information about what a structure contains, where components are located, and how large they are.

Important Information in Structural Drawings

1. TitleIdentifies what the drawing represents, such as a floor plan or foundation plan.

2. Scale Shows the relationship between the drawing size and the actual size.

3. DimensionsProvide measurements of structural elements and spaces.

4. SymbolsRepresent structural and architectural elements without drawing every detail.

5. Labels and NotesProvide additional information about materials, components, locations, or construction requirements.

6. Grid LinesReference lines used to help identify and locate structural elements such as columns and beams.

Common Structural Drawing Views

Plan View Shows the structure as viewed from above.

Elevation View Shows the structure from a particular side.

Section View Shows the internal arrangement of a structure through a cut-through view.

Detail View Shows a specific part of the structure at a larger or more detailed scale.

What Students Should Look For

When reading a simple structural drawing, pay attention to:

What is being shown.

Where each component is located.

How large each component is.

Which symbols represent specific elements.

What notes provide additional information.

What scale is being used.

Lesson Discussion

What is a Floor Plan?

A floor plan is a scaled drawing that shows the arrangement of spaces and features of a building as viewed from above. It provides information about the layout, dimensions, and location of different elements.

Basic Parts of a Floor Plan

1. WallsShow the boundaries and divisions of rooms and spaces.

2. DoorsShow entrances and indicate the direction in which a door opens.

3. WindowsShow openings in walls that allow light and ventilation.

4. Rooms and SpacesRepresent areas such as bedrooms, living rooms, kitchens, bathrooms, and other spaces.

5. DimensionsShow the measurements of rooms, walls, openings, and distances.

6. Labels Identify the purpose or name of each room or space.

7. StairsShow the location and direction of movement between different levels.

Important Features

Scale Allows the building to be represented proportionally on paper.

SymbolsProvide a simple way to represent doors, windows, stairs, and other elements.

DimensionsProvide accurate measurements of spaces and building components.

Room Labels Identify the function of each space.

Learning Objectives

Lesson Discussion

What are Structural Elements?

Structural elements are the parts of a building or structure that support, carry, and transfer loads. They work together to provide stability and strength.

Common Structural Elements

1. Foundation The lowest part of a structure that transfers loads from the building to the ground.

2. Column A vertical structural member that carries loads from beams, slabs, and upper parts of a structure toward the foundation.

3. Beam A horizontal structural member that carries loads and transfers them to columns or other supports.

4. Slab A flat structural element that commonly forms floors or roofs and transfers loads to supporting members.

5. Structural Wall A wall designed to carry and transfer loads while also providing enclosure or separation.

6. Roof Structure The structural components that support the roof covering and transfer roof loads to the supporting structure.

Basic Load Transfer

Structural elements work together to transfer loads:

Slab → Beam → Column → Foundation → Ground

This basic load path helps students understand how forces move through a structure.
