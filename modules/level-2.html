Drafting Standards and Geometry

Lesson 4: Drafting Lettering and Symbols

Learning Objectives

After this lesson, students should be able to:

Identify the purpose of drafting lettering.

Understand basic lettering standards.

Recognize common structural drafting symbols.

Understand how lettering and symbols communicate information in drawings.

Lesson Discussion

What is Drafting Lettering?

Drafting lettering is the use of clear, uniform, and readable letters and numbers in technical drawings. It is used for labels, notes, dimensions, titles, and other important information.

Characteristics of Good Drafting Lettering

Good technical lettering should be:

Clear – easy to read.

Uniform – letters have consistent size and appearance.

Neat – free from unnecessary marks.

Legible – information can be understood easily.

Consistent – the same lettering style is maintained throughout the drawing.

Drafting Symbols

Symbols are simplified graphical representations used to communicate information without drawing every detail.

Common examples include:

Door Symbol – represents the location and opening of a door.

Window Symbol – represents a window in a plan.

Column Symbol – represents the location of a structural column.

Beam Symbol – indicates a structural beam.

Stair Symbol – represents stairs and their direction.

Section Symbol – identifies where a section view is taken.

North Arrow – indicates the direction of north on a drawing.

Lettering in Dimensions

Numbers and letters are also used with dimensions to communicate the size and location of structural elements.

For example:

3000 mm → indicates a length of 3,000 millimeters.

200 × 300 mm → may indicate the dimensions of a structural member, depending on the drawing.

Lesson 5: Measurement and Scale

Learning Objectives

After this lesson, students should be able to:

Understand basic measurements used in Structural Drafting.

Identify common units of measurement.

Understand the concept of scale.

Distinguish between actual size and drawing size.

Lesson Discussion

What is Measurement?

Measurement is the process of determining the size, length, width, height, or distance of an object. Accurate measurements are important in Structural Drafting because structural drawings must represent dimensions correctly.

Common Units of Measurement

Millimeter (mm) – commonly used for detailed measurements in technical drawings.

Centimeter (cm) – used for smaller measurements.

Meter (m) – commonly used for larger dimensions such as room or building sizes.

What is Scale?

Scale is the relationship between the size of an object in a drawing and its actual size.

Because buildings and structures are often too large to draw at their actual size, they are represented using a smaller or larger proportional scale.

Common Drawing Scales

1:1 – Full Scale The drawing is the same size as the actual object.

1:2 – Half Scale The drawing is half the size of the actual object.

1:10 – Reduced Scale1 unit on the drawing represents 10 units in the actual object.

1:100 – Reduced Scale1 unit on the drawing represents 100 units in the actual object.

Example

If a wall is 5 meters long and the drawing uses a 1:100 scale:

5 m actual length → 5 cm on the drawing

The important idea is that the drawing remains proportional to the actual structure.

Lesson 6: Basic Geometric Construction

Learning Objectives

After this lesson, students should be able to:

Identify basic geometric shapes used in drafting.

Understand basic geometric elements such as lines, angles, and curves.

Understand how geometric construction is applied in Structural Drafting.

Recognize the importance of accuracy in geometric construction.

Lesson Discussion

What is Geometric Construction?

Geometric construction is the process of creating accurate shapes, lines, angles, and curves using drafting tools. It is an important foundation for creating technical and structural drawings.

Basic Geometric Elements

1. Point A precise location in a drawing.

2. Line A straight path between two points.

3. Angle The space formed by two lines that meet at a point.

4. Arc A curved portion of a circle.

5. Circle A closed curved shape in which all points are the same distance from the center.

Common Geometric Shapes

Rectangle – a four-sided shape with four right angles; commonly seen in floor plans and structural layouts.

Square – a four-sided shape with equal sides and four right angles.

Triangle – a three-sided shape used in various structural and geometric representations.

Circle – commonly used to represent circular elements or components.

Application in Structural Drafting

Geometric construction is used when creating:

Floor plan layouts

Structural components

Openings and circular elements

Angles and connections

Building outlines

Construction details

Importance of Accuracy

Accurate geometric construction helps ensure that:

Lines meet correctly.

Angles are properly represented.

Shapes maintain their intended proportions.

Structural drawings are clear and consistent.
