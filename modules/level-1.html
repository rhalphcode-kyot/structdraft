What is Structural Drafting?

Structural Drafting is the process of creating technical drawings that represent the design, dimensions, and details of a structure. It uses lines, symbols, measurements, notes, and scales to communicate how structural components are arranged.

Importance of Structural Drafting

Structural drafting helps to:

Communicate structural information clearly.

Show the size, shape, location, and arrangement of structural components.

Provide drawings that can be used as references during construction.

Reduce errors by providing accurate technical information.

Help professionals understand how different parts of a structure are connected.

Basic Structural Components

1. Foundation The part of a structure that transfers loads to the ground.

2. Column A vertical structural member that carries loads from upper parts of a structure toward the foundation.

3. Beam A horizontal structural member that carries loads and transfers them to supports such as columns or walls.

4. Slab A flat structural element commonly used for floors and roofs.

5. Wall A vertical element that may enclose spaces or, when designed as a structural wall, support loads.

Basic Types of Structural Drawings

Floor Plan – shows the arrangement of spaces and structural elements as viewed from above.

Elevation – shows the exterior or interior appearance of a structure from a particular direction.

Section – shows the inside of a structure as if it were cut vertically.

Detail Drawing – provides a more specific and enlarged view of a particular structural part or connection.

Lesson 2: Drafting Tools and Materials

Learning Objectives

After this lesson, students should be able to:

Identify common drafting tools and materials.

Understand the purpose of each tool.

Understand the proper use of basic drafting equipment.

Lesson Discussion

1. T-Square Used mainly for drawing straight horizontal lines and as a guide for triangles.

2. Triangle Used to draw vertical lines and specific angles, such as 30°, 45°, 60°, and 90°.

3. Ruler Used for measuring and drawing straight lines.

4. Scale Used to measure and create drawings according to a specific scale or proportion.

5. Compass Used to draw circles and arcs.

6. Protractor Used to measure and construct angles.

7. Pencil Used for drawing lines, details, and lettering. Different pencil grades produce different line darkness.

8. Eraser Used to remove unwanted pencil marks and correct drawing errors.

9. Drawing Board Provides a flat and stable surface for creating technical drawings.

10. Drafting Tape Used to secure the drawing paper to the drawing board.

Pencil Grades

2H / H – harder pencils that create lighter lines.

HB – medium hardness, commonly used for general drafting.

B – softer pencils that create darker lines.

What are Drafting Lines?

Drafting lines are used in technical drawings to represent objects, edges, measurements, and other important information. Each type of line has a specific purpose.
